Scry

A Video.js skin that turns the video into a seeing-stone.

Drag the timeline, or drag left and right on the picture.

Things to try

Drag
Scrub the timeline, or drag anywhere on the picture. The video rolls up into the stone and plays through it as you move, with the timeline bent around the rim. Let go and it opens on the new frame.
Hover
Rest the pointer on the timeline and a small stone shows that moment. Hold still for a second and it sharpens. The video keeps playing.
Skip
Use the arrow keys, the number keys, or double-click either side of the picture. Long skips pass through the stone; short ones just cut.
Wait
When the video stalls, the stone fills with smoke. When it ends, the stone holds the opening frame. Press it to start again.

How it works

Scrubbing a video normally shows you a strip of thumbnails. Those thumbnails come from a storyboard: one image, published alongside the video, with a small frame every few seconds.

Scry takes the same image and works out how the picture moves between each frame and the next. It then uses that motion to push one frame into the other, so a few dozen stills look like footage. Where the video cuts to a different shot there is no motion to follow, and the frames dissolve instead.

All of this is shader code running in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no model to download.

Still a normal player

Under the stone are the standard Video.js controls: play, seek, volume, captions, speed, quality, picture-in-picture and fullscreen. They all work from the keyboard and on touch, and the timeline is still a labelled slider for screen readers.

If you have reduced motion turned on, or your browser has no WebGL2, the stone never appears. Seeking cuts as usual and the preview is a plain thumbnail.
